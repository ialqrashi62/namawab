## 🎯 Clinical Authority (CMO)

### 1. Space & Dive Medicine

**Indications:**
- Commercial / military / private pilots
- Commercial / military / scientific / recreational divers
- Astronauts
- Hyperbaric chamber patients
- Submarine crews
- Altitude medicine (mountaineers, high-altitude cities)
- Subaquatic / submarine work

**Key conditions:**
- Decompression sickness ("the bends")
- Arterial gas embolism
- Barotrauma (otic, sinus, pulmonary)
- Nitrogen narcosis
- Oxygen toxicity (CNS, pulmonary)
- Hypoxia / hyperoxia
- Hypothermia
- Dysbaric osteonecrosis
- High-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE)
- High-altitude cerebral edema (HACE)
- Acute mountain sickness (AMS)
- Spatial disorientation
- G-force induced loss of consciousness (G-LOC)

**Standards:**
- IMCA (International Marine Contractors Association)
- US Navy Dive Tables
- PADI / SSI / NAUI recreational
- COMEX commercial
- NOAA scientific
- FAA / ICAO aviation medical
- NASA space medicine
- Civil Aviation Authority (KSA GACA)

**Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) — additional indications beyond dive:**
- CO poisoning
- Smoke inhalation
- Necrotizing fasciitis
- Clostridial myonecrosis (gas gangrene)
- Compromised skin grafts/flaps
- Radiation tissue damage
- Diabetic foot (Wagner III+)
- Idiopathic sudden sensorineural hearing loss
- Central retinal artery occlusion

**HBOT chamber types:**
- Monoplace (single patient)
- Multiplace (multiple patients, attendant inside)

**HBOT schedule (varies by indication):**
- 2-3 ATA, 90-120 min
- 5-7 days/week
- 20-40 sessions typical

### 2. Sleep Disorders Center (Polysomnography)

**Indications:**
- Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)
- Central sleep apnea (CSA)
- Mixed / complex sleep apnea
- Hypoventilation syndromes (obesity hypoventilation)
- Periodic limb movement disorder (PLMD)
- Restless legs syndrome (RLS)
- Narcolepsy
- Idiopathic hypersomnia
- Parasomnias (REM behavior disorder, NREM parasomnias, sleepwalking)
- Circadian rhythm disorders
- Insomnia

**Polysomnography (PSG) setup:**
- EEG (10-20 system, Fp1, Fp2, C3, C4, O1, O2, A1, A2, plus EOG, EMG chin, EMG legs)
- ECG
- Pulse oximetry
- Respiratory effort (thoracic, abdominal belts)
- Airflow (nasal pressure, thermistor)
- Body position
- Snore microphone
- Video

**PSG scored in 30-second epochs per AASM Manual:**

| Sleep stage | EEG | EOG | EMG |
|---|---|---|---|
| W (wake) | Alpha | Eye blinks | High |
| N1 (light) | Theta | Slow | Decreasing |
| N2 | Spindles, K-complexes | — | — |
| N3 (deep) | Delta | — | — |
| R (REM) | Low voltage, sawtooth | REMs | Atonia |

**Events:**
- Apnea: ≥ 10s cessation of airflow
  - Obstructive: continued effort
  - Central: no effort
  - Mixed: starts central, ends obstructive
- Hypopnea: ≥ 30% reduction in airflow + 3-4% desaturation OR arousal
- RERA (respiratory effort-related arousal)
- Arousal (3-15s EEG shift to alpha)
- Snoring
- Limb movement

**AHI (Apnea-Hypopnea Index) / RDI (Respiratory Disturbance Index):**
- AHI = (apneas + hypopneas) / hours of sleep
- Normal: < 5
- Mild: 5-15
- Moderate: 15-30
- Severe: > 30

**OSA treatment (PAP):**
- CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure)
- APAP (auto-titrating)
- BiPAP (bilevel)
- ASV (adaptive servo-ventilation, for CSA)
- Pressure: 4-20 cmH2O
- Titration: in-lab, auto, or empirical

**Other OSA treatment:**
- Oral appliance (mandibular advancement)
- Positional therapy
- Weight loss
- Upper airway stimulation (Inspire)
- Surgery (UPPP, MMA)

**CPAP compliance:** > 4h/night, > 70% of nights

### 3. Epilepsy Monitoring Unit (EMU)

**Indications:**
- Diagnostic (paroxysmal events, non-epileptic vs epileptic)
- Presurgical evaluation
- Medication adjustment
- VNS / RNS / DBS evaluation
- ECT (electroconvulsive therapy)
- Status epilepticus

**EMU setup:**
- Continuous video-EEG (24/7, 1-7 days)
- Reduced anti-seizure meds to capture events
- Sleep deprivation
- Photoparoxysmal response testing
- Hyperventilation
- Ictal SPECT
- MEG (magnetoencephalography)
- 7T MRI (if available)

**EMU safety:**
- 1:1 nursing (24h)
- IV access
- Emergency medications at bedside
- Suction, O2
- Sitter (no bathroom alone, no shower)
- Bed height low
- Padding
- No smoking

**Seizure types (ILAE 2017 classification):**
- Focal onset
  - Aware vs impaired awareness
  - Motor vs non-motor
  - Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic
- Generalized onset
  - Motor (tonic-clonic, clonic, tonic, myoclonic, atonic, epileptic spasms)
  - Non-motor (absence)
- Unknown onset

**Epilepsy syndromes (age-related):**
- Self-limited neonatal seizures
- Febrile seizures
- BECTS (benign epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes)
- Childhood absence epilepsy
- Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy
- Lennox-Gastaut
- Dravet
- Rasmussen encephalitis
- Temporal lobe epilepsy (most common adult)

**Anti-seizure meds (ASM):**
- Narrow-spectrum: carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin, gabapentin
- Broad-spectrum: valproate, levetiracetam, lamotrigine, topiramate, zonisamide, lacosamide, perampanel
- Special: vigabatrin (infantile spasms), felbamate (refractory), CBD (Dravet, LGS)

**Status epilepticus:**
- Tonic-clonic > 5 min
- Treatment: 1st line benzodiazepine (lorazepam IV 4mg, midazolam IM/buccal/intranasal, diazepam IV/rectal)
- 2nd line: levetiracetam 60 mg/kg, fosphenytoin 20 mg/kg, valproate 40 mg/kg
- 3rd line (refractory): midazolam, propofol, pentobarbital infusion
- Super-refractory: anesthetic doses > 24h

**Epilepsy surgery:**
- Resective: anterior temporal lobectomy (most common), lesionectomy
- Laser ablation (MRgLITT)
- RNS (responsive neurostimulation)
- VNS (vagus nerve stimulation)
- DBS (deep brain stimulation, anterior nucleus of thalamus)

### 7. Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)

**Indications (FDA-approved):**
- Parkinson disease
- Essential tremor
- Dystonia (primary, segmental)
- Drug-resistant epilepsy (anterior nucleus thalamus)
- OCD (under HDE)

**Targets:**
- STN (subthalamic nucleus) — Parkinson
- GPi (globus pallidus interna) — Parkinson, dystonia
- VIM (ventralis intermedius) — tremor
- ANT (anterior nucleus thalamus) — epilepsy
- NAC / VC/VS (nucleus accumbens / ventral capsule) — OCD, depression

**Workflow:**
1. Patient selection (movement disorder neurologist, neurosurgery)
2. Pre-op imaging (MRI, fMRI, DTI tractography)
3. Frame-based or frameless stereotactic
4. Microelectrode recording
5. Macroelectrode test stimulation
6. Implantable pulse generator (IPG, "battery")
7. Programming (1-2 months post-op, multiple sessions)
8. Medication adjustment
9. Long-term follow-up

**Programming parameters:**
- Active contacts
- Amplitude (V or mA)
- Pulse width (μs)
- Frequency (Hz)
- Cycling mode

**Complications:**
- Hemorrhage (1-2%)
- Infection (3-5%)
- Lead fracture
- IPG malfunction
- Migration
- Stimulation-induced side effects (paresthesia, dysarthria, mood changes)

### 8. Nuclear Medicine Therapy

**I-131 (radioactive iodine) therapy:**
- Hyperthyroidism (Graves, toxic nodular)
- Differentiated thyroid cancer (after total thyroidectomy)
- Doses: low (≤30 mCi) for hyperthyroidism, high (50-200 mCi) for cancer

**Lu-177 dotatate (Lutathera):**
- Neuroendocrine tumors (GEP-NETs)

**Lu-177 PSMA (Pluvicto):**
- Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC)

**Ra-223 (Xofigo):**
- Bone metastases (castration-resistant prostate cancer)

**Y-90 radioembolization (SIR-Spheres, TheraSphere):**
- Liver tumors (HCC, mets)

**I-131 MIBG:**
- Neuroblastoma, pheochromocytoma

**Tb-161 (emerging):**
- PSMA + DOTATATE

**Radiation safety:**
- Patient isolation (high-dose I-131)
- Time, distance, shielding
- ALARA
- Pregnancy screening
- Contamination control
- Waste handling

### 9. Cryotherapy / Cryosurgery

**Mechanism:**
- Rapid freezing → intracellular ice → cell death
- Cryogens: liquid nitrogen (-196°C), liquid N2O (-89°C), argon gas

**Applications:**
- Dermatology: actinic keratosis, warts, skin cancer (BCC, SCC in situ), keloids
- Gynecology: cervical, endometrial (cryoablation)
- Urology: prostate (focal therapy, primary)
- Cardiology: AF ablation (cryoballoon)
- Oncology: liver, lung, kidney, bone (palliative)
- Ophthalmology: retinal cryopexy
- ENT: tonsillectomy

**Cryoprobe types:**
- Spray
- Closed-tip probe
- Balloon
- Catheter-based

**Cycles:** freeze-thaw-freeze (more effective)

### 10. Confocal Laser Endomicroscopy (CLE)

**Mechanism:**
- Real-time in vivo microscopy at cellular/subcellular resolution
- Probe-based (pCLE) or through endoscope (eCLE)
- IV fluorescein contrast
- "Optical biopsy"

**Applications:**
- GI: Barrett's esophagus, colon polyp characterization, IBD, gastric, biliary, pancreatic cystic lesions
- Pulmonary: distal lung, mediastinum
- GU: bladder (cystoscopy), ureter
- Others: brain (intraop), breast (ductoscopy)

**Advantages:**
- Real-time in vivo histology
- Reduce need for biopsy
- Margin assessment (intraop)
- Surveillance (Barrett's, IBD)

### 11. Pharmacogenomics

**Mechanism:**
- Genetic variation → drug response
- CYP450 polymorphisms most important
- HLA-B*57:01 (abacavir), HLA-B*15:02 (carbamazepine)

**Common tests:**
| Gene | Drugs affected |
|---|---|
| CYP2D6 | Codeine, tramadol, tamoxifen, SSRIs, antipsychotics |
| CYP2C19 | Clopidogrel, PPIs, voriconazole, sertraline |
| CYP2C9 | Warfarin, phenytoin, NSAIDs |
| VKORC1 | Warfarin |
| TPMT | Azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine |
| NUDT15 | Azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine |
| DPYD | Fluoropyrimidines (5-FU, capecitabine) |
| UGT1A1 | Irinotecan |
| HLA-B*57:01 | Abacavir |
| HLA-B*15:02 | Carbamazepine (Asian) |
| HLA-A*31:01 | Carbamazepine (European) |
| SLCO1B1 | Statins (myopathy) |
| IFNL3 (IL28B) | Hepatitis C |
| G6PD | Rasburicase, primaquine, dapsone, sulfonamides |

**Implementation:**
- Pre-emptive testing (panel)
- Reactive testing (after adverse event)
- EHR alerts (CDS)
- Pharmacist consult
- Test interpretation by clinical pharmacist or genetics counselor

**Guidelines:**
- CPIC (Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium)
- DPWG (Dutch Pharmacogenetics Working Group)
- PharmGKB
- FDA table of pharmacogenomic biomarkers

### 12. Nanomedicine

**Mechanisms:**
- Drug delivery (targeted nanoparticles)
- Imaging (nanoparticle contrast agents)
- Diagnostics (lab-on-chip, biosensors)
- Theranostics (combined therapy + imaging)
- Tissue engineering (scaffolds)
- Wound healing (silver nanoparticles, etc.)

**Examples:**
- Doxil (liposomal doxorubicin)
- Abraxane (nab-paclitaxel)
- Onpattro (patisiran, siRNA-lipid nanoparticle)
- Comirnaty (mRNA-lipid nanoparticle)
- Ferumoxytol (iron oxide MRI contrast, also iron supplement)
- Silver nanoparticles (wound dressings)
- Gold nanoshells (photothermal therapy)

**Future:**
- CRISPR delivery
- Smart nanoparticles
- Self-assembling biomaterials
- Targeted CNS delivery (BBB crossing)

**Safety:**
- Long-term toxicity
- Bioaccumulation
- Renal/hepatic clearance
- Environmental impact
- Regulatory (FDA, EMA, SFDA nanomedicine guidance)
