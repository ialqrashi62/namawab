# OBG-001 Top 10 Conditions + Workflows

## 1. Pre-eclampsia

**Definition:** New-onset HTN (BP ≥140/90) after 20 weeks + proteinuria or end-organ damage

**Severe features:**
- BP ≥160/110 (on 2 occasions, 4h apart)
- Proteinuria >5g/24h
- Thrombocytopenia (platelets <100)
- Elevated LFTs (AST/ALT >2x ULN)
- Renal insufficiency (creatinine >1.1)
- Pulmonary edema
- New-onset headache or visual disturbances

**Management:**
- MgSO4 4g IV loading, then 1-2 g/h maintenance
- Antihypertensives (labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine)
- Delivery (only definitive cure)
- Timing: 37+ weeks for mild, 34+ for severe, immediate if eclampsia/HELLP

## 2. Eclampsia

**Definition:** Seizures in pregnancy + pre-eclampsia features

**Management:**
- ABCs (airway, breathing, circulation)
- MgSO4 4g IV over 5-20 min, then 1-2 g/h
- If seizure continues: MgSO4 2g IV, then consider benzodiazepine
- BP control
- Delivery after stabilization (regardless of gestational age)
- MgSO4 continued 24h postpartum

## 3. Placental Abruption

**Definition:** Premature separation of placenta from uterus

**Risk factors:** HTN, trauma, cocaine, smoking, prior abruption

**Signs:**
- Painful vaginal bleeding (may be concealed)
- Tender, rigid uterus
- Fetal distress (bradycardia, late decelerations)
- DIC (in severe cases)

**Management:**
- Type & crossmatch 4 units PRBC
- IV access x2 (large bore)
- Continuous fetal monitoring
- If stable + term + mild: delivery (usually induction)
- If unstable or severe: emergency C-section
- Coagulopathy: FFP, platelets, cryoprecipitate

## 4. Placenta Previa

**Definition:** Placenta covering or near cervical os

**Signs:**
- PAINLESS vaginal bleeding (typically 3rd trimester)
- No tenderness on exam (NO digital exam!)
- Ultrasound: confirms diagnosis

**Management:**
- NO digital vaginal exam (risk of hemorrhage)
- Type & crossmatch
- Antenatal corticosteroids if <34 weeks
- Tocolysis controversial
- Delivery by C-section at 36-37 weeks (or earlier if bleeding)

## 5. Ectopic Pregnancy

**Definition:** Pregnancy outside uterine cavity (most commonly fallopian tube)

**Risk factors:** Prior ectopic, PID, tubal surgery, IUD, smoking, IVF

**Signs:**
- Abdominal pain (often unilateral)
- Vaginal bleeding (light)
- Amenorrhea (6-8 weeks)
- hCG positive + empty uterus on US
- Free fluid in cul-de-sac (rupture)
- Hypotension (if rupture)

**Management:**
- Stable + small + low hCG: methotrexate (MTX) 50 mg/m² IM
- Stable + larger or higher hCG: surgical (laparoscopy)
- Unstable + rupture: emergency laparotomy
- Follow-up hCG until undetectable

## 6. Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

**Definition:** >500mL EBL (vaginal) or >1000mL (C-section) within 24h

**Causes (4 Ts):** Tone (atony), Trauma (lacerations), Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (coagulopathy)

**Management (HAEMOSTASIS algorithm):**
1. **H**elp (call for help)
2. **A**ssess + resuscitate (2 large-bore IVs, crystalloid, type & crossmatch)
3. **E**stablish etiology + **E**vacuate uterus
4. **M**assage uterus
5. **O**xytocin 10 units IV bolus, then 40 units in 1L NS at 250 mL/h
6. **S**hift to second-line uterotonics (methylergometrine, carboprost, misoprostol)
7. **T**amponade (Bakri balloon)
8. **A**rterial ligation (uterine, ovarian)
9. **C**ompression sutures (B-Lynch)
10. **H**ysterectomy (last resort)

## 7. HELLP Syndrome

**Definition:**
- **H**emolysis (LDH >600, schistocytes)
- **E**levated **L**iver enzymes (AST/ALT >70)
- **L**ow **P**latelets (<100)

**Management:**
- Stabilize (MgSO4, BP control)
- Corticosteroids if <34 weeks
- Delivery (mode depends on cervical status + gestational age + severity)
- ICU if severe
- Platelet transfusion if <50 (or <20 for vaginal, <50 for C-section)
- Monitor for DIC, hepatic rupture, renal failure

## 8. Gestational Diabetes

**Screening:**
- 24-28 weeks: 75g OGTT (1h + 2h)
- Diagnostic: fasting ≥92, 1h ≥180, 2h ≥153

**Management:**
- Medical nutrition therapy
- Glucose monitoring 4x/day
- Insulin if not controlled (fasting >95, 2h postprandial >120)
- Fetal monitoring (growth US, NST in 3rd trimester)
- Delivery: 39-40 weeks (or earlier if complications)

## 9. Preterm Labor

**Definition:** Regular contractions + cervical change before 37 weeks

**Management:**
- Confirm with fetal fibronectin (if available) or transvaginal US (cervical length)
- Tocolysis (nifedipine, atosiban) if <34 weeks (48h for steroids)
- Antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone 12mg IM x2, 24h apart)
- Magnesium sulfate (neuroprotection) if <32 weeks
- GBS prophylaxis (penicillin) if indicated
- Antibiotics (ampicillin + erythromycin) for latency

## 10. Ovarian Torsion

**Risk factors:** Ovarian cyst or mass (typically >5 cm)

**Signs:**
- Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain
- Nausea/vomiting
- Adnexal mass on US
- Decreased blood flow on Doppler (late sign)

**Management:**
- EMERGENT surgery (laparoscopy)
- Detorsion (save ovary if possible)
- Cystectomy or oophorectomy (depending on findings)
- Time critical (every hour = more tissue loss)

## Time Targets

| Condition | Time Target |
|-----------|-------------|
| Pre-eclampsia severe | MgSO4 within 30 min |
| Eclampsia | MgSO4 within 5 min of seizure |
| PPH | First uterotonic within 5 min |
| Ectopic rupture | OR within 15 min |
| Cord prolapse | Delivery within 30 min |
| Ovarian torsion | OR within 1-2 hours |
| Preterm labor | Steroids within 7 days of presentation |
